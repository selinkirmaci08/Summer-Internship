Key Findings
Structural Findings

Six Structural Properties Shape Modelling Decisions

Weekly seasonality is the dominant structure
ACF lag-6 = 0.582 (pre-regime), lag-7 = 0.308 (post-regime). Same-day-of-week lookback features are the primary signal source. Day-to-day carry-forward (lag-1 = 0.325) is secondary.
Persistent downward trend: −28.7% over 10 years
OLS slope = −0.039 units/day. ADF p = 0.0005 (stationary — not a random walk but trend-stationary). A year_trend feature is required to capture the ongoing level decline.
Structural regime change 2020-06-01
Store switched from 6-day (Mon closed) to 7-day trading. Pre-regime mean 318.3 → post-regime 285.0 (−10.5%). The seasonal period changes from 6 to 7 trading days/week. Rolling-origin CV must straddle this boundary.
50 zero-demand days — not randomly distributed
24 in 2020 (COVID lockdown March–May); remainder are likely Belgian public holidays. Zero-demand days must stay in the series (they affect multi-day forecasts) but are flagged via an is_zero_demand binary feature.
2014 data is severely sparse: 64 trading days vs ~250 expected
Cause unknown; confirmed in protocol.json. CV folds spanning 2013–2015 will have uneven coverage. Annual seasonality features computed on 2014 will be unreliable. Feature engineering must handle this explicitly.
Medium/long-horizon autocorrelation is strong
ACF lag-30 = 0.500, lag-60 = 0.465, lag-90 = 0.418. Seasonal median naive predictors at h=30–90 are a strong baseline. Models must include same-DOW rolling features at these horizons to be competitive.

Key constraint

Seasonal period is 6 trading days/week before 2020-06-01 (Mon closed) and 7 after. Lag and rolling-window features must use k=6 pre-regime and k=7 post-regime for same-DOW lookbacks — a universal k=7 will cross DOW boundaries in the pre-regime period.

Regime note

Monday (n=31) appears only from 2020-06-01 onward. Pre-regime Mondays are excluded from the series — they would be structural zeros. The post-regime Monday mean (241.5) represents approximately 4–5 observations in CV fold 3 training, making Monday the most data-sparse DOW for model learning.

The dominant weekly seasonality (ACF lag-6 = 0.582) means that same-day-of-week seasonal median features are both the strongest naive baseline and the most important feature type for ML models. Any model without explicit DOW encoding will systematically mis-forecast the 164-unit spread between Sunday (416.5) and Friday (252.5).

The ACF shows two distinct features: a sharp drop from lag-1 (0.325) to lag-2, followed by a recovery at lag-6 (0.582). This is the fingerprint of within-week DOW variance dominating the series. The significant ACF at lags 30 (0.500), 60 (0.465), and 90 (0.418) supports building same-DOW seasonal median features at all six forecast horizons — these are not noise, they reflect genuine monthly and quarterly demand cycles in the bakery's trading pattern.

Metric recommendation: MAE

MAPE is unsafe (division by zero on 50 zero-demand days). RMSE would disproportionately penalise the few high-quantity outlier days (max=691). MAE aligns with operational planning: each unit of error has equal cost regardless of the day's baseline demand level.

Zero-demand handling

Zero-demand days must stay in the series. Dropping them creates date-join gaps that break multi-horizon target construction (a target at D+h may fall on a zero-demand day and is a real, forecasting-relevant value). Add is_zero_demand binary as a feature.

Rules & Cautions
Binding Constraints for Downstream Skills

Durable Rules and Methodological Cautions

Binding Rules (must not change)

  • Per-horizon targets by DATE JOIN only — row.date + h calendar days. Never positional shift. Trading-day gaps break positional alignment → silent target corruption.
  • Seasonal period is 6 pre-2020-06-01, 7 from 2020-06-01 — same-DOW lags and rolling windows must use k=6 (pre-regime) and k=7 (post-regime). A universal k=7 crosses DOW boundaries pre-regime.
  • Rolling-origin CV must straddle the 2020-06-01 regime boundary — CV restricted to pre-regime period fails to test post-regime generalisation; the 2021+ holdout is entirely post-regime.
  • Weather temporal column is Date_time — not 'date', 'timestamp', or 'Date'. Using the wrong column produces all-null weather features without raising an error.
  • VerlofConcurrent.csv: use VC1 only, sep=';' — VC2 is an exact VC1 duplicate. Competitor CSV uses semicolons; commas appear in data fields.
  • Deliverydata must not be used as features — wholesale channel; delivery anticipates demand → potential leakage; confirmed across multiple runs.
  • Weather aggregation: mean/max/min — never sum — Visual Crossing API repeats daily totals in each hourly row; summing produces 24× inflated values.

Domain Research Flags (open questions)

  • Belgian public holidays — map to zero-demand days in training data; verify against calendar before treating them as modellable events.
  • Gentse Feesten (mid-July Ghent festival) — check for systematic demand spike or dip pattern; annual event within the training window.
  • COVID-19 — Belgium lockdown from 2020-03-18; 24 zero-demand days in 2020 are lockdown closures, not normal seasonality. Rolling-origin CV construction must handle this correctly.
  • 2014 sparse data — only 64 trading days; cause unknown. May need exclusion from some CV folds or flagging as an anomaly year for annual seasonality features.
CV h=90 constraint

h=90 has only 5 scoreable validation rows in fold 3 (dataset ends 2020-12-31; last usable h=90 origin is ~2020-10-02). Training must use all 3 CV folds when scoring h=90 candidates — fold 3 alone gives unreliable MAE estimates from 5 rows.
