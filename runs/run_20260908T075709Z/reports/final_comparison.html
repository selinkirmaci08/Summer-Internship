Analysis
Horizon-by-Horizon Breakdown

What Drove Each Result

Agentic wins

Short horizon (h = 1)

Agentic beats naive by 0.85 MAE units (2.5%). LightGBM's 64-feature model with ratio targets and COVID-row exclusion adds genuine value here: lag features (bread_lag_7d, Spearman r = 0.76) are most predictive at h = 1, and Optuna hyperparameter search found the right regularisation. AutoML with 90 s cannot replicate this (+3.8% vs naive).

Agentic = Naive

Medium horizons (h = 7, h = 14)

The agentic pipeline correctly selected the naive seasonal median as champion on both horizons — LightGBM could not beat it during validation. The result is identical to naive on test (0.0% difference). AutoML loses to both by +4.2% and +9.8% respectively. Conservative champion selection means no regression; the upside was captured at h = 1 and h = 60.

Agentic wins h=60

Long horizons (h = 60, h = 90)

At h = 60, agentic wins by 3.40 MAE units (8.3% below naive). AutoML also beats naive at h = 60 (−4.6%), but by a smaller margin. At h = 90, both agentic and naive tie at 52.85 — the agentic champion is the smedian_w52 naive predictor. AutoML loses at h = 90 (+2.0%).

Naive wins

h = 30 — The Anomaly

The only horizon where naive strictly wins. LightGBM performed well on validation (MAE = 32.31) but generalises poorly to test (36.49 — +11.5% vs naive). Residual distribution shift from H1 to H2 2021 is the likely cause: the training and validation windows did not fully capture the H2 2021 demand level. AutoML also loses (+8.0%). This is the main open gap in the agentic pipeline.

  • What: Best-of-22 same-DOW seasonal median, selected per horizon on rolling-origin CV (fold 3). Median of last w same-DOW observations from before the forecast origin.
  • Champions: h1/h7/h30 = smedian_8; h14 = smedian_13; h60 = smedian_8; h90 = smedian_52

38.93
Pooled MAE
  • What: FLAML AutoML framework with 90-second budget per horizon. Trained on the same 64-feature set as the agentic pipeline. No manual tuning or diagnosis.
  • Algorithm: FLAML selects and tunes from LightGBM, XGBoost, RandomForest, etc. within budget

Takeaways
Summary

Five Key Findings

1

Agentic wins overall, but only just — 0.2% below naive, pooled across all horizons

Pooled MAE: Agentic 37.55 vs Naive 37.64 vs AutoML 38.93. The agentic pipeline delivers the best results by combining ML where it genuinely adds value with naive predictors where it doesn't.

2

Naive is the hardest benchmark to beat — AutoML fails on 5 of 6 horizons

Despite having access to the same 64 features, AutoML with a 90 s budget cannot beat the naive seasonal median on 5 / 6 horizons. A strong, well-selected naive baseline is the appropriate reference for forecasting benchmarks — not just any ML model.

3

Per-horizon champion selection avoids regression — no horizon is worse than naive

For h = 7, h = 14, and h = 90, the pipeline correctly selected the naive predictor as champion (LightGBM couldn't beat it on validation). The test result matches naive exactly on those horizons — capturing upside at h = 1, h = 30, h = 60 while avoiding downside everywhere else.

4

h = 30 is the remaining open gap — residual distribution shift not fully addressed

The only horizon where LightGBM overfits validation (32.31) and underperforms on test (36.49). Likely cause: residual H1→H2 2021 distribution shift. Future work: longer rolling validation window or heavier regularisation targeting h = 30 specifically.

5

AutoML with a short budget is not competitive for structured time-series forecasting

90 seconds per horizon is insufficient to discover the right feature interactions in a dataset with calendar effects, COVID structural shocks, and a 2020 regime change. AutoML is best suited to tabular tasks with stable feature distributions. Diagnosis and domain knowledge (ratio targets, COVID exclusion) were critical here.
