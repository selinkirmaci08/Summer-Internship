Root Cause Analysis
Diagnosis

Two Compounding Problems Prevent Model Learning

Problem 1 — COVID Training Contamination

  • 57 COVID lockdown rows in training (2020-03-18 to 2020-05-03, demand ≈ 0)
  • These rows have mean demand ≈ 263.6 vs. normal 318.5 — extreme downward gradient signal
  • LightGBM regression_l1 on the 95-row validation set fires early stopping at 12 trees
  • At 12 trees, only ~60 splits possible across 38 features — lag/calendar features cannot be used
Evidence: bread_lag_7d Spearman r = 0.76 with target (strong signal), yet permutation importance ≈ 0. The feature is available but the model is too shallow to use it.

Problem 2 — Regime-Change Underfitting

  • 30 post-regime training rows (1.1% of 2,713 total) — all from 2020-06-01 to 2020-06-30
  • Post-regime Mondays in training: only 4–5 examples
  • Validation: 95 rows, all post-regime — includes 14 Mondays
  • Monday demand is a structurally new pattern (shop was closed Mondays pre-2020-06-01)
Evidence: 4–5 post-regime Monday training examples vs. 14 Monday validation rows. No model can reliably learn the Monday pattern from 4–5 examples.
Why smedian_w4 is immune to both problems: It uses only the 4 most recent same-DOW observations. This naturally excludes COVID-period observations (pre-lockdown same-DOW recency) and adapts quickly to post-regime demand levels as new Monday observations accumulate. The naive predictor is not "simple" — it is perfectly calibrated to this distribution shift.
Additional oracle checks:
Ratio oracle (h=1): naive × mean_noncovid_ratio → val MAE = 58.166 vs. floor 31.26. A constant ratio predictor fails by 26.9 units — ratio target alone is insufficient without informative features.
ARIMA/SARIMA: pooled val MAE = 73.35 vs. floor 35.58. Forecasting 89–184 steps ahead from training end is fundamentally unreliable. Classical TS is eliminated on this split.

Three Ranked Hypotheses — Ordered by Confidence and Cost

RANK 1
Medium confidence Moderate cost

Ratio target + COVID row exclusion

FE adds ratio_h{h} = bread_quantity_h{h} / anchor_h{h} (clipped [0.1, 4.0]). Training filters is_covid_lockdown_period==1 rows. Addresses BOTH root causes simultaneously: ratio removes level shift, COVID exclusion removes gradient contamination.

Anchors: smedian_w4 for h≤30 · smedian_w26 for h≥60
Falsification: If val MAE ≥ 31.263 for h=1 after both changes → reject
The additive residual target (y − anchor) was tried in iter006 and failed: train residual mean = +13.45 vs. val residual mean = −13.15 for h=1, a 26.6-unit distribution shift that persists in residuals. The multiplicative ratio (y / anchor) is stationary for non-COVID rows (oracle: train ratio mean 1.0616 vs. val ratio mean 0.9724 — much closer). The combination of ratio target and COVID exclusion is therefore needed to remove both the COVID gradient contamination and the additive level shift simultaneously.
RANK 2
Medium confidence Cheap

COVID rows excluded from training only

Filter is_covid_lockdown_period==1 rows from LightGBM training (no FE change needed). Tests whether COVID contamination alone explains the 12-tree early stopping failure. Reduces training from 2,713 to ~2,656 rows.

Rationale: Cheapest test — no FE required
Falsification: If val MAE ≥ 31.263 after COVID exclusion → COVID rows are not the primary cause; look at regime underfitting instead
Ranked 2 (below ratio target) because COVID exclusion alone may still leave the level-shift problem: train_noncovid_mean ≈ 318 vs. val_mean ≈ 306. The model trained without COVID rows would predict at the non-COVID historical level, which is still different from the post-regime validation level. If this alone beats the floor, the ratio target (rank 1) is unnecessary. Testing rank 2 before rank 1 isolates the COVID contamination hypothesis cleanly.
RANK 3
Low confidence Cheap

Rolling training window (2019–2020)

Filter training to date ≥ 2019-01-01 AND is_covid_lockdown_period==0, giving ~320 rows. Use aggressive regularisation (max_depth=3, min_child_samples=20, L2=1.0) to prevent overfitting. Tests whether recency beats breadth.

Risk: 320 training rows for 38 features is very small for LightGBM — high overfitting risk
Falsification: If val MAE ≥ 31.263 → dataset too small for tree-based learning
Addresses both COVID contamination and the level trend simultaneously (recent normal data has mean demand closer to the validation period). Ranked 3 because lowest confidence: 320 training rows for 38 features is very small for LightGBM. Aggressive regularisation may prevent overfitting but will also prevent learning. However it is cheap to run alongside rank 2 in the same training iteration.
Alternative if all three fail: Accept smedian_w4 (h=1–30) and smedian_w26 (h=60–90) as per-horizon champions. Proceed to end_model with the naive predictor. Document that ML-as-regression cannot beat the naive predictor given COVID-distorted training distribution and limited post-regime data.
