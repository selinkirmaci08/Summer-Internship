Nothing learned beat the naive on the holdout

Agentic pipeline run_20260904T140437Z, two AutoML arms and two zero-parameter references, scored on the external holdout they were all withheld from. Primary window 2021 (2,139 scored (target date, h) pairs); stress window 2022 – 2023-02-08 (324 pairs).

1. Verdict

Against AutoML with no feature engineering — the pipeline loses. The reference AutoML arm is the one that does no feature engineering at all: raw columns, a single shift(h), no lags, no calendar terms, no rolling statistics. It is what the pipeline's whole EDA → preprocess → feature-engineering chain has to justify itself against. It scores MAE 48.59 on 2021 against the agentic champion's 57.59 — the champion wins 1/6 horizons. On the stress window, 54.42 against 68.62. Source: automl-runs/automl-baseline/outputs/naive_automl/preds_holdout_primary.csv and holdout_eval/outputs/preds_holdout_primary__agentic_shipped_calendar.csv.
Matched on training data, that gap closes to a tie. The shipped champion was fitted on origins to 2018-10-04; every AutoML arm was fitted on the whole training partition to 2020-12-31. Refitting the same six specifications on the same rows — no re-tuning, per contracts/end_model.json — gives 49.09 against no-feature-engineering AutoML's 48.59 on 2021. That is a dead heat. Read together with the verdict above: most of the apparent gap to AutoML was training data, and what remains is that eight iterations of agent-directed feature engineering bought nothing measurable over doing none at all.
Against naive — every learned system loses. The seasonal naive scores 39.84 on 2021. No learned system reaches it: no-feature-engineering AutoML 48.59, the agentic champion 57.59, the agentic refit 49.09. The champion beats naive at 0 of 6 horizons. A 52-week seasonal median — also zero parameters — beats everything at 38.57, and on the stress window wins all 6 horizons at 25.67.
Why — the models forecast the right shape at the wrong level. Removing each system's constant bias cuts the agentic champion's stress-window error from 68.62 to 43.04, and the refit's from 60.26 to 35.85 — at which point its day-to-day shape is as good as naive's (35.80). Bias grows every year (+32 → +54 → +60) while naive's stays flat (+3 → -9 → -0). This is distribution shift, not a feature-engineering failure.

All six systems were withheld from the holdout: the agentic run directory contains no path to it, and the AutoML training table stops at 2020-12-31 with zero rows after. The two agentic rows marked row-offset h are a sensitivity check, not separate systems — the boosters were trained against targets h rows ahead on a frame with pre-2020-06 Mondays deleted, so their native h=30 is 35 calendar days. Scoring them both ways costs 0.23 MAE, so the convention is not what decides this comparison.

Alignment record

  • Primary: 362 target dates × 6 horizons = 2,172 pairs; 18 excluded by the trading-day rule (actual = 0 or absent); 2,154 scored; 2,139 where every system produced a prediction. Largest single-system loss to the join is 15 rows (0.7%), under the 5% threshold.
  • Stress: 786 scored pairs but only 324 complete across systems — 2022 carries 92 of ~310 expected trading days, so for most (target, h) pairs the calendar origin is simply not an observed date. The stress table is therefore a smaller, gappier comparison and is reported separately for that reason.
  • Horizon grids match exactly (1, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90). No horizon was paired or dropped.
  • One date lost: 2021-11-02's only export is truncated mid-file. Parsing its intact prefix would under-count bread and bias the target low, so the date is excluded from every system.

5. Skill against naive — no learned system reaches parity

Every learned series sits above the parity line at every horizon on the primary window. The seasonal median dips below it from h=14 onward, which is the clearest statement the report can make: a one-line rule with no fitting is the best forecaster in this comparison.

Grouped permutation importance, computed on the validation partition, never on the holdout — attribution on the test set would spend the one thing it is for. Each figure is the increase in validation MAE when the whole group is shuffled together; the masking gap is the grouped effect minus the sum of the members' solo effects.

Only the Fourier calendar terms carry the model (+2.46 MAE when permuted). The seven lag features — the ones the run's EDA justified as the core signal — score -1.44: permuting them improves validation accuracy. Weather (12 features) and competitor (7 features) are indistinguishable from zero and can be dropped. year_centered, the only trend term, scores exactly 0.00 — the boosters never split on it once.

That last number explains section 6. A model with no usable trend term cannot follow demand downward, so it reproduces the weekday shape at the level it was fitted on, and the bias grows with every year of drift. It also explains why the refit helps: three more years of rows moved the frozen level closer to the holdout, worth 8.50 MAE on 2021, without changing anything else.

8. Limits

  • The lag features are aliased. The run measured autocorrelation in row offsets on a frame with Mondays deleted, where a week is six rows. Its "monthly signal at lag-30, r=0.601" is the weekly signal: on the calendar, lag-30-days has r=0.022 while every multiple of 7 sits near 0.6. Four of the seven lag features point at weekday-misaligned offsets. This is a defect in the run, not in the scoring.
  • The stress window is thin. 324 complete pairs, and 2022 is missing two-thirds of its expected trading days. Directionally consistent with 2021; not independently strong.
  • The refit is not the shipped artifact. It is what contracts/end_model.json now requires and what the run would have produced under the current contract. Reported alongside, never in place of, the model the run actually froze.
  • No system was tuned for this holdout, and none may be after seeing it. These numbers are spent the moment they inform a modelling change.
